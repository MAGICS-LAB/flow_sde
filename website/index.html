The framework

Stochastic inference.
One RL framework.

A unified view connects generative-model fine-tuning and flow-policy optimization through the inner MDP induced by SDE inference.

Reward fine-tuning of flow models

Each stochastic inference transition is an action in an inner MDP. A terminal reward guides policy-gradient updates, including GRPO, without an external environment.

Flow policies in an environment

The inner inference MDP is composed with the environment MDP. An entire denoising chain produces the action chunk executed in the environment.

Our instantiation

FMPO-CPPO learns from the full inference chain.

ChainPPO combines a clipped surrogate for the complete inference chain with an auxiliary clipped surrogate for individual inference steps. Log-ratios are normalized by the action-chunk dimension.

JFMPO-CPPO = Jchain + α Jstep

The experiments use α = 0.25, a chain clip of 0.02, a step clip of 0.01, and noise schedule λt = a(1 − t). The normalized ratios are tempered surrogates, as explained in Section 3.3.

Theoretical foundations

When does stochastic
training transfer?

The analysis separates the inference mismatch from the reward improvement achieved by RL.

Correctness · Theorem 4.1

Small flow-matching error controls the inference gap.

TV(pT, qT) ≤ O(√ε)

When the learned velocity approximates the reference flow well, SDE and ODE inference induce close terminal distributions. Here ε is the square root of the integrated squared velocity error.

Performance transfer · Theorem 4.2

SDE improvements transfer beyond the mismatch penalty.

JODE(θk+1) ≥ JODE(θk) + Δk − 2R(δk + δk+1)

For a bounded terminal reward, deployment improves when the SDE reward gain exceeds the distributional mismatch penalties at the old and new parameters.

These guarantees hold under the approximation and regularity assumptions in the paper, with early stopping time T < 1. Better pretraining and controlled fine-tuning help keep the mismatch small.

Abstract

We develop a unified framework for flow-based reinforcement learning (RL) grounded in diffusion–flow duality and establish its theoretical foundations. The framework enables RL for deterministic flow matching inference by introducing diffusion stochastic inference dynamics that supports exploration while admitting deterministic deployment. We show that our framework subsumes several existing flow-based RL methods as special cases and inspire effective new methods. Then we provide theoretical foundations for the frameworks by establishing the correctness and performance transfer guarantee. Specifically, we prove that the policies optimized under stochastic dynamics close to deterministic dynamics at deployment when the pretrained model is well-trained. Moreover, we show that the policy improvement achieved under training-time SDE inference transfers to deployment-time ODE inference. Experiments on synthetic and continuous-control tasks validate the theory and demonstrate the proposed algorithms’ effectiveness.
